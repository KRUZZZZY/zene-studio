# Q050-231.1 - Cross-track masking report (stage 1 of 3, L08, R-mixing-7)

**Unit.** `mixer.masking_report` compares the audio of selected mixer channels. It returns the frequency ranges they
share, with a proposed cut on the lower-priority channel (front / middle / back) for each. It is read only. Stage 2
(applying a proposal as one undo step) and stage 3 (the full musician workflow) come later. This stage adds nothing
those stages would have to undo: each proposal already carries the exact `mixer.channel_eq` arguments it stands for
(`strip_eq`).

## What it does

- **Source audio.** A channel's audio is the long-term spectrum of the unmuted audio clips on the unmuted tracks that
  play into it (`track->mixerChannelModel()`), over each clip's played window (`SampleClip::sampleWindow()`). The
  channel's own strip-EQ response (`mixerstrip::eqResponseDb`) is applied at each band centre. So changing a channel's
  EQ changes what it holds (`theStripEqShapesTheChannel`). This is not `mixer.eq_compare`, which compares EQ settings
  only.
- **Analysis** (`src/core/ControlCommandsMixerMasking.cpp`):
  - Frames are 4096-point Hann windows, at most 64 per clip, spaced evenly over the window. The FFT is the existing
    `detection::detail::fftRadix2`.
  - Power is averaged into 24 log bands from 40 Hz to 16 kHz.
  - A band counts for a channel when it is above -70 dB and within `threshold_db` (3..40, default 12) of that
    channel's loudest band.
  - Every run of adjacent bands that two channels both hold is one clash.
  - Everything is plain arithmetic on fixed frames, and the channels are taken in mixer order whatever order they were
    named in. The same audio, EQ and priorities therefore give byte-identical replies.
- **Proposal:**
  - `target` is the lower-priority channel. At equal priority it is the channel quieter in the range (by more than
    0.5 dB); when the levels tie too, it is the later channel.
  - `cut_db` = -(3 + (protected level - target level) / 4), clamped to -1..-6 and rounded to 0.1 dB.
  - `strip_eq` holds the `mixer.channel_eq` arguments for the cut. The low shelf is used for a range that ends at or
    below 200 Hz, the high shelf for one that starts at or above 6 kHz, and otherwise the mid bell at the range's
    centre (100..10000 Hz). Each value is added to the target's current setting and clamped to ±18 dB.
- **Reply:**
  - `channels`: for each channel, `channel`, `name`, `priority`, `clips` read, `peak_db`, and `unanalysed_tracks`
    (tracks that play into it with no audio clip, such as instrument tracks).
  - `clashes`: for each clash, `a`, `b`, `low_hz`, `high_hz`, `centre_hz`, `a_db`, `b_db`, `target`, `protect`,
    `reason`, `cut_db` and `strip_eq`.
  - Also `threshold_db`, `low_hz`, `high_hz` and `bands`.
- **Refusals** (`InvalidArgs`): fewer than 2 or more than 16 distinct channels; a priority that is not front, middle
  or back; a priority for a channel that is not among `channels`; an unknown channel id.

## Realtime and threads

The handler runs on the control (main) thread, like every registry command. It reads the clips' shared, immutable
`SampleBuffer` data and the EQ models' values. It never touches a mixer channel's period buffer, takes no audio-thread
lock, and adds nothing to the audio callback. All of its allocation (FFT scratch, JSON) happens in the handler. Local
TSan does not run on this box. The race argument is by design: the only data shared with the audio thread is the
sample buffers, which are read-only once they are installed, and the EQ `FloatModel` values, which are read the same
way `mixer.eq_compare` already reads them.

## Known limits

- Only audio clips are analysed. Instrument tracks are listed in `unanalysed_tracks` and are not rendered, so their
  content does not count.
- The audio is taken before the inserts and the fader. Only the strip EQ is applied. Channels that send into a
  selected channel are not followed.
- The spectrum covers the whole of each clip's window, regardless of when clips play against each other in time.
  Two parts that never overlap in time can still be reported as a clash.
- The strip EQ has one mid bell of fixed width. Two proposals for the same target's mid band would compete; choosing
  between them is stage 2's job.
- Priorities are not saved with the project in this stage.
