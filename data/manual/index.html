Zene Studio

Zene Studio is a music workstation built around one main window: the arrangement in the middle, the browser on the left, the Inspector on the right and the editors and the mixer below. Three views arrange these panels for the job at hand - Arrange, Mix and Edit (Alt+1 to Alt+3). Everything a click does is also a named command, so a script, an agent or a controller can do it too. Changes to the project can be undone (Edit > Undo, or Window > Undo History); actions outside it - files written to disk, messages sent to hardware - cannot.

Hover over a control to see what it does; press Shift+F1 (Help > What's This?) and click a control for its full description. Help > Command Reference lists every command with its arguments, and the Command Palette finds any menu item or command by name: Enter runs it, or opens its settings when it has any, Shift+Enter runs it with its defaults, and a command that cannot be undone is marked; Help > Keyboard Shortcuts lists every shortcut, and Project tools > Customise > Keyboard Shortcuts changes them.

The window

  • Header - transport (play, stop, record), tempo, time signature, position, the master level, the transpose, the CPU load and the loudness readout. Right-click it to choose which buttons it shows.
  • Footer - a switch for each panel, grouped by where it opens, Tools (every tool that opens in a window of its own: controller rack, project notes, scales and keymaps, undo history, tempo map and more), and the views. Narrower, the switches show icons only, and the ones that still don't fit are under More; the header's right-hand buttons do the same. An area that holds several panels shows a tab for each: click one, or use the arrow keys, to open it, and use the cross to hide the area.
  • Browser - devices, presets, samples and projects. Search is the first control and filters the current tab as you type; Up and Down move the highlight, Enter loads the highlighted result (Shift+Enter puts a sample on a sample track) and Ctrl+F jumps to it. A click or Space previews, and the preview stops when you pick another result, change the query, tab or location, or leave the Browser. The Search source finds files across the library: every word must match (kick 808 finds items with both), kick OR snare, -loop excludes, "bass drum" is a phrase, and tag: kind: name: path: ext: narrow a word. Save a search as a collection to keep it; Project Media lists every file the project uses. A file dragged out of the Browser - from its list or a search result - carries the file itself as well, so a plug-in editor's window, the desktop or a file manager that takes files receives the file, while Zene's own drop targets (a sample clip, a track, the session grid) load it as they always did.
  • Inspector - the selected track, clip or mixer channel: its settings, its sends and inserts, its take lanes.
  • Device panel - the devices of the selected track or channel as cards, with the rack's macro knobs.

Arranging

Drag clips to move them; drag their edges to trim. Choose a range with the Range tool (Navigate > Arranger Tool > Range: drag over the tracks; the Smart tool does the same from a clip's upper half), then split, delete, crop, fade or bounce it from Navigate > Range. A clip dragged onto another trims it (Navigate > Overlap chooses trim, crossfade or free), and before you let go the arranger shows what will be cut and what the clip snaps to. A locked clip can be selected but not moved, trimmed or deleted - a padlock marks it. Sections, markers and tempo have lanes of their own under the bar ruler (right-click them to show or hide one); a pinch or Ctrl+wheel zooms around the pointer, the zoom keys around the selection or the play position. Ctrl+wheel zooms smoothly - a touchpad as finely as you turn it. Where labels would overlap they are shortened or left out; hover to read them. Double-click a section or a marker in its lane to zoom to it. Navigate holds the zoom, markers, ripple and overlap modes; Alt+arrows nudge and select clips. Drop a MIDI file or an audio file on the arranger - from a plug-in editor's window, the desktop or a file manager - to make a clip of it: on a track's lane, on a clip or on a track's name (the clip starts where you let go, on the grid), or on the empty space below the tracks, which adds a new track for it. A MIDI file becomes one MIDI clip on an instrument track (all its channels and tracks together; its tempo is not used); an audio file becomes a sample clip on a sample track. One drop is one undo step. A file that cannot be used - missing, empty, damaged, not MIDI or audio this build reads, a MIDI file that would run past the end of the song, or the wrong kind for the track - is refused with a message in the status bar and nothing in the project changes. Only the first file of several dropped together is used. Audio that a plug-in leaves in the system's temporary folder is copied into the clip, so it survives the plug-in deleting it; other audio stays a link to its file. The same action is the agent command clip.import_file. Sample clips show fade handles near their top corners and, with Navigate > Show Transients, their transients. Project > Sections names the song's parts (verse, chorus) as bands over the ruler and selects one as the range; Clip List finds a clip by name; Next and Previous Transient move the play position through the audio's hits. A clip's Sample Editor... (its menu) raises or lowers by 3 dB, silences, deletes, reverses, fades or crops a selection of its audio; Split to stems (a sample clip's menu) separates its audio into stems on tracks of their own. A sample clip's menu removes its silent stretches, crops its sample to what it plays, makes it unique, converts its melody to MIDI notes or splits it at the play position. A crossfade's two sides can have their own lengths and shapes. Moving a folder track moves every clip inside it. A clip's Snap Point (its menu) is the place inside it that lands on the grid when you drag it - a downbeat after a pickup. Navigate > Show Navigator puts the whole song in a strip under the arranger: click or drag in it to scroll, turn the wheel to zoom; it names the sections and shows the markers, the loop and the play position, and Alt-click or a double-click on one zooms the arranger to it. Navigate > Second Tool on a Modifier Key gives Alt, Ctrl or Shift a tool of its own while held. Track > Versions keeps several versions of a track's clips and switches between them. An audio clip's Speed menu ramps its speed (the pitch going with it) or adds a tape start or a tape stop.

Splits and trims can land on an exact sample, and with Navigate > Snap Cuts to Zero Crossings on, the knife and an edge drag move to the nearest zero crossing so the cut does not click. A track's menu adds a spacer below it (Add Spacer Below): an empty band that separates groups of tracks and moves with the track.

Sample Editor (a clip's menu, Sample Editor...): drag across the waveform to select a range, or drag the [ and ] handles at its edges. Start, End and Length show the range in the unit Units picks - time from the source's start, bars and beats at the song's tempo from the clip's start, or samples - and can be typed into (Enter applies; a suffix such as s, smp or bars overrides the unit) or stepped with Up and Down. End is the first frame after the range. Ctrl+wheel zooms the waveform around the pointer, the wheel scrolls it, and Zoom In, Zoom Out, Zoom to Selection and Show All do the same from the keyboard. Each operation edits only the range the fields show, as one undo step; the line under the buttons says what was done, or why it was refused.

Edit > Tempo Map... lists the song's tempo and metre changes: add, remove and ramp them, or import and export them as a Standard MIDI File.

Edit > Progression... lists the named progressions and spells each one out in the selected scale and key; Write puts the chosen progression into the selected MIDI clip as notes, as one undo step.

Automation can be switched off for reading or writing across the whole song (Project > Automation Read / Write), and writes can be limited to the punch region. A control's trim offsets its automation without changing the curve.

Editing notes

Double-click a MIDI clip to open it in the note editor. The tools follow FL Studio's piano roll: P draw, B paint, D delete, T mute, C slice, E select, Y play, Z zooms to the selection (1-7 also pick them). With any tool the right button deletes every note it crosses (one undo step); a double-click on a note opens its properties; Ctrl selects (Ctrl+Shift adds), Shift and Ctrl lock pitch and timing while dragging, Alt bypasses the grid and Alt+wheel changes velocity; a new note takes the last note's length; Ctrl+C, X, V and B copy, cut, paste and duplicate; the lanes below edit velocity, pan, chance, timbre, pressure, micro-pitch, release velocity, velocity spread and repeats, and the clip's controller curves. The Notes menu quantises, transposes, scales, strums and transforms; Pitch Curve draws a bend on each note. Notes > Tools inverts, arpeggiates, flams, repeats, moves notes a degree up or down in the key, makes them follow the chord track, writes a Euclidean rhythm, moves notes to another track, splits a drum loop into a track per key and detects the key. Group keeps notes together when you select them; Lyrics lays syllables on the notes ("Hal-le-lu-jah"). Play On chooses the passes of the loop a note plays on; the Repeats lane plays a note several times inside its length; the Velocity Spread lane varies its velocity each time. Articulation picks one of the track's articulations (Track > Articulations... sets the keyswitch, program or controller each one sends). New Note Length fixes the length a click draws; Audio Behind the Notes shows an audio clip's waveform under them. The controller lane can also hold program changes.

Each lane under the notes names what it shows and its scale - for example Velocity, 0 % to 200 %, with the values at its top, middle and bottom on the right. Point at a stem and the lane reads out that note's value and the value at the pointer; while nothing is pointed at, the header shows the selected notes' values. Controller lanes keep MIDI's 0 to 127 (pitch bend: -8192 to +8191 around its centre) and read out the curve's value at the pointer's position. Selected notes have square heads; a note whose key is folded away has a hollow one. A muted note has a dashed outline, a note that may not play a dotted one and its chance in its label, and a very short note can be clicked a little beside it.

Project > Global Swing delays every off-beat 16th (or 8th) of the song; Chase Notes sounds a note playback starts, loops or jumps into the middle of, or one already under the playhead when its track or clip is unmuted; MIDI Playback Offset starts every note a little later, to line up a slow instrument.

Mixing

Each strip has its trim, polarity, mono and pan mode (balance, dual or width), sends with their own pan, inserts and a meter line with the clip light and the gain reduction. Right-click a strip and open More for its Strip, Listen and Mixer View menus: show or hide sections, change the strip width, show only some channels, switch the meter between peak, RMS and pre-fader. VCA groups can be nested and choose which of level, mute and solo reach their members. Solo can be exclusive or additive (Mixer View > Solo Mode). A return's Mixer View menu has Show Sends to Here on Faders: every channel's fader then sets its send to that return (Channel Faders goes back). A strip can swap its left and right sides; a send can be switched off and on again and keeps its level; Create a Return makes a bus fed by the channel; Adjust Channels Together moves several faders by the same amount. Project > Pan Law chooses how much a centred signal is cut (none, 3, 4.5 or 6 dB), Control Room > Mono Check folds what you hear to mono, Order the Mixer by Tracks lines the channels up with the tracks, and Find a Channel searches the names. Each send in the Inspector has a meter beside it, and instrument and sample track headers show their level. Mixer View > Compare Channel EQ draws two channels' EQ over each other. The Control Room (Project menu) switches between three sets of speakers, each with its own trim, and dims, mutes or checks the mix in mono - on what you hear only, never on an export. A channel's "Play Previews Here" sends the browser's previews to it instead of the master.

A channel's Hardware I/O menu sends it to your interface's outputs as well as the mix, builds up to four cue (headphone) mixes with their own sends, and adds a hardware insert: send to an output, return from an input, with the round trip measured by a ping and compensated. Interface Channels names your interface's inputs and outputs and pairs them; the names are kept per device, not per project. Its Surround menu sets a channel to LCR, quad, 5.1 or 7.1: the Surround Panner places a channel's sends into it on a 2-D room with an LFE send and spread, and a stereo master or a stereo export gets the standard downmix. Extra channels need a backend with more than two outputs (JACK or ALSA). With a surround master, the WAV export's Channels row can write it as it is - Surround: one channel per speaker (6 for 5.1, 8 for 7.1) in the standard WAV order with its speaker mask; Stereo, the default, folds it with the downmix.

The mode bar across the top of the mixer shows and sets how it is laid out and what mode it is in. View: Mix shows levels, pan and processing; Routing shows, on every strip, where its signal comes from and where it goes, by channel name, with each send's amount and the hardware output. Density: Compact keeps only the essentials (no inserts or macro knobs, routing on one line); Expanded shows every section turned on in Mixer View. Strips never shrink below their width; use Show to filter channels, or scroll. The mode buttons name what the faders drive, the solo mode (exclusive or additive), who is listening (AFL or PFL) and where the master plays and in what speaker format; click one to change it. Every strip has a badge saying what it is (Master, Return, Group, Track, Channel or VCA, plus its surround format) and a line for the modes it is in (AFL/PFL, Solo safe, Surround pan, Cue, HW insert). Routing Matrix... opens every channel against every channel; filter the senders by name, and double-click or press Enter on a cell to make or change that send.

Devices

Add a device from the browser or the rack's menu; right-click a device to insert another before or after it, copy it or its whole chain to other channels, or compare two settings (A/B). Racks hold parallel chains, macros (each mapping with its own range and curve) and key or velocity zones; Wrap into Rack moves a chain into a rack chain. Plug-in windows have a header with presets, and an effect's also has bypass and A/B. Favourite, hide or file plug-ins into folders from the effect picker or the plug-in manager (Project > Plug-in Manager); Preferred Plug-in Format lists a plug-in installed in several formats once. Project tools > Plug-in Modules... lists every file the scan found with its path, its per-file status and the error it failed with, and quarantines or releases one from its row (Edit > Plugin Quarantine adds and releases entries too). A device card's dot lights while the device processes audio. The rack menu bypasses every device, switches them all back on, removes the bypassed ones and opens an XY pad that plays two macros at once. Modulators can be random, smooth random, a step sequence or a retriggered envelope as well as an LFO; a modulator can run a formula of its own (sin(2*pi*t), tri(t) * rand(x)...) and restart on every note a track plays.

A rack can split its input between its parallel chains (Rack > Split the Input): by frequency band, mid and side, or left and right - the chains add back to the whole. A device's menu finds, renames and randomises its parameters (a renamed parameter keeps its own name for macros and modulators), blends its A and B settings, folds its card to the header, plays only what the device changes (the card's Δ button), and sets compatibility options: the generic editor instead of the plug-in's window, keep running on silence, a latency override, the editor zoom. The Device panel shows the level of the channel its devices play into. Native devices: De-esser, Transient Shaper, Vocoder (the voice on the sidechain), Console (Mixer > Console Across the Mix puts one on every channel), Dynamic EQ (bands that move with the level, on the mid or the side alone), Convolution Reverb (Load Impulse Response... from its menu) and Recorder (records what passes it; its menu puts the last take on a new track). An audio clip's Process menu prints a chain's effects onto the clip.

Each device card has an Actions button (⋯), and the same menu opens on a right-click: Bypass, Open Editor, Presets, A/B, Replace With…, Move Left, Move Right and Remove, then Insert, Copy, Parameters and Hosting. Under the card's header a line says the device's state in words: Bypassed, which A/B setting you hear, the delay it adds, whether its plug-in host crashed or was quarantined, and the preset it last loaded or saved. When the chain changes, the cards that did not change stay where they are. Add device, Insert and Replace open the device picker. With a mixer channel selected, Add device fills the channel's selected parallel chain (Rack > Route to); an empty parallel chain has its own Add device... button under its heading. Start typing to filter it by name, vendor, format or type. The arrow keys move through the list, Enter adds the highlighted device and Escape cancels. Each row shows the device's type, format, vendor and state; favourites come first.

The Inspector keeps its place as you move through a project: a section that still shows the same track, clip or channel is left as it is, and a changed section keeps your keyboard focus and scroll position. When the selected notes differ, a field says 'Multiple values', and a key, velocity or length you type there is given to every selected note; a typed start moves the first note there and keeps the spacing. Volumes read in dB, velocities and pan in %, and times in the time format. Rename a track or a mixer channel by typing in its name field.

Recording

Arm a track with the record button in its header (or Record > Arm All Audio Tracks), choose its input from its Record menu and press Record. Record > Count-In and Pre-Roll starts the take bars early; Record Mode chooses for MIDI between overdub (the notes already in the clip stay) and replace (a recorded note erases the notes it overlaps, at any pitch - notes only; controllers and automation are kept); the header's Rec mode readout shows which. Positions and lengths read in bars and beats - right-click a position, or use the Tempo Map's picker, for ticks or time; any of them can be typed. After a looped recording, Split into Loop Passes (the sample clip's menu) puts every pass on its own take lane: drag along a lane in the take-lanes view to comp that range, double-click to cycle the take (or Record > Next Take Here). Retrospective capture keeps what you played without recording - save it as a clip or a take afterwards. Record > Punch In Here and Punch Out Here set the punch region at the play position while you record; Metronome Sound and Level chooses the click, its level, the accent and whether it clicks only while recording. Track > Takes and Comping moves the edge between two takes, or comps several tracks (a kit) at once. The header's Rec left readout shows how much recording time the disk's free space still allows at what the armed routes write (record.remaining - "-" when a volume cannot be measured, "0m" when the budget is spent); scripts and agents ask the same command. A recording route can take the master or a channel instead of the input (record.route_source), before or after the channel's devices or fader - if you also monitor what you record, lower the level first: a signal recorded from what it feeds can howl. Track > MIDI Out sends an instrument track's notes to a hardware synth; record its audio back on an audio track that monitors the input.

An instrument track's Instrument Setup menu - on its header menu, the Inspector's More menu and its instrument's device card - holds what a multi-output, multitimbral or layered instrument needs. Output Buses lists a plug-in's extra outputs: route one to a mixer channel, or make a channel for each. MIDI Parts sends the notes in a key and velocity range on a MIDI channel of their own, so one track plays several parts of a multitimbral plug-in; edit a part in place or remove it. Layers > Layer Rack stacks more instruments on the track, each with Mute, Solo, a Level and its own window, and Layer Zones picks which layers a note plays on. Per-Voice Modulation makes a modulator run once per note of the track and binds it to a parameter of the instrument; Modulation Assignments changes a depth or removes a binding.

The launcher

Show the launcher from the footer's Clip Launcher button, View > Clip Launcher or Alt+L. Each cell plays a pattern or an audio file; drop a sample on a cell to fill it. Each column is headed by its track's name. A cell's play mark shows its state - ▷ stopped, ▷… queued, ▶ playing - and a scene's mark shows its row's, so you can see what will launch. Dragging a sample over a filled cell shows "Replace" before you let go; after the drop, Undo Replace (or Edit > Undo) puts the old clip back. Cells launch on the next bar (or the cell's own quantisation) in trigger, gate, toggle or repeat mode, and scenes launch a row. Launcher > Launch Scene 1-8 can be given keys (Project tools > Customise > Keyboard Shortcuts); Launcher > Controller binds the notes of a MIDI controller to cells and scenes. Launcher > Record What I Played Into a Cell puts what you just played (with Keep Listening on) into a cell as a new pattern. A Gate cell plays while you hold it. Right-click a cell for its Follow Action Chain (the action after it, its chance and timing) and Jump To (move a playing cell inside its clip); right-click a scene to play scenes in order or launch a folder's cells together; the grid's menu captures the playing cells as a new scene and keeps a setlist of songs to step through. A marker can run a command when playback crosses it (marker.set 'action').

For a show, View > Perform Mode (or Launcher > Perform Mode) opens a window made to be read from a distance: the setlist's song and the next one, Play and Stop, the bar and beat, the tempo with - and + (each an undoable change), the scene playing and the scene that plays next, one big button per scene, what each track is playing, and a large Stop All. Its keys: 1-9 launch scenes 1-9, Up and Down choose the next scene, Enter launches it and moves on to the one after, Space starts or stops, Escape stops every clip, Page Down and Page Up change song (asking about unsaved work first). With a controller connected (Launcher > Controller > Connect...), press Learn MIDI and then a scene button or Stop All: the next note you play is bound to it.

A sub-scene is part of a scene - some of its tracks - that you can launch on its own, for example only the drums of the chorus. Ctrl+click the cells (or Ctrl+Space on a focused cell) to select them, then right-click and choose Sub-scenes > Make a Sub-scene From the Selection; it takes the cells of one scene. The scene's Sub-scenes menu then launches, renames and deletes it. Its cells launch as the scene would launch them, on the same bar lines, and the other tracks play on.

Ableton Link. The Ableton Link button in the arranger's toolbar (also Project > Sync) plays the project in time with Ableton Live, Bitwig, Reason and every other Ableton Link app on your network or on this computer. The tempo is shared both ways - a tempo another app sets is not an undo step - and while the song plays it stays on the session's beat grid: started on its own, it waits for the session's next bar (the quantum, 4 beats unless Project > Sync > Quantum changes it). With Project > Sync > Share Start and Stop on, play and stop in any app that shares them start and stop the others. The button shows how many apps are connected and the session tempo; link.get_state has the rest.

Projects

Projects keep their history: Project > Revisions restores or compares an earlier state. Collect Media copies every file a project uses beside it; Project > Hand Off writes a folder someone else can open with a list of the devices to install first. Import Tracks copies tracks from another project; Save as Template keeps a project to start from.

Project > New Document starts a production, mastering or live project from its own template (your own type.mpt in the templates folder wins); the window title names a mastering or live document. Project > Other Projects opens another project as a read-only reference beside this one and copies its tracks, clips or devices across, and switches to a recent project - keeping the one you left open as the reference if you like. Project > Import AAF... brings in an AAF from another editor: its audio tracks, clips, fades, clip gain and markers (embedded audio is extracted beside the project; OMF is not read). View > Video Window shows a project's video in time with the transport, with a strip of its frames over the arranger; the video's sound can be extracted to an audio track. Edit > Collaborate... lets someone on your network (or across the internet, where your router allows it) join the project: you share an invite code, every edit is sent to the others, and a remote invite gives a phone or another machine the transport and faders only. Nothing listens until you host, and you are asked first.

Scripts, agents and controllers

Every command is reachable over the control socket (JSON-RPC) and from the MCP bridge; Lua scripts (zene.command) reach all of them except those that change your own configuration or start other scripts (shortcuts, workflows, scripts, crash reporting, telemetry). The socket accepts connections only from your own user account (it is created readable and writable by you alone); anything connected to it can do whatever the menus can - save, render, delete - so connect only programs you trust. A controller can drive the remote-control pages (eight knobs following the selected device), a Mackie Control surface, or any parameter through MIDI learn.

Customising and the Window menu

Project tools > Customise > Theme chooses (from the next start) the palette (dark, high contrast or light) the interface zoom and accessible mode, and edits the theme's colours one by one - export a theme to share it, import one, or reset to the palette's own. Window > Layouts (also Customise > Window Layouts) saves the panels' arrangement under a name and brings it back; it says what a layout keeps (the view, the open, pinned and floating panels and their sizes - not the zoom, the scroll or the selection). Save never overwrites a name in use; Replace asks first. A project can remember a layout of its own, put back when it opens; your own layout comes back when you open a project without one, or start a new one; the Workflows menu (and Customise > Workflows...) records a run of commands and plays it again, shared by every project or kept with each (Keep Workflows). Drag a panel's header out of the window to float it - on another monitor if you like - and drag it back onto its place, or press Dock, to return it (Ctrl+Shift+F floats or docks the focused panel). Customise > Settings finds any setting by a word, backs all of them up to a file and restores them, and keeps named settings presets (folder paths never travel in a preset or a backup). While a workflow is being recorded the header says so - "Recording workflow ..." with the steps so far - and its Stop button stops and saves it. A workflow that stops at a step that fails says which step and why, which steps applied (they stay applied; undo each with Edit > Undo) and which did not run, in a report that stays open: Retry Step runs that step again, Skip Step and Continue runs the rest without it (both are in the Workflows menu too).

The Window menu holds the track manager (every track in one list: mute, solo, hide, rename, colour, icon, move, remove, and how much processing its mixer channel takes), a large clock, a tuner (it listens to the input, or to the master), the undo history (double-click a command, or select it and press Undo to Here, to go back to before it), memory locations (a named place with its range and zoom) and saved selections (a named range and clips) - each says what it keeps; Replace Memory Location and Replace Saved Selection ask before overwriting. The floating tools (also under the footer's Tools) open where you last left them. The View menu opens the floating windows: Controller Rack (Ctrl+6), Project Notes (Ctrl+7), Scales and Keymaps and the Classic Piano Roll. The Window menu also keeps the main window on top, cascades or tiles the floating windows, shows a cross-hair cursor across the arranger, and moves the keyboard focus from panel to panel with F6 and Shift+F6. A pinch on a touch screen or a trackpad zooms the arranger.

Access

Help > Appearance sets the interface zoom (one control sizes text, icons and controls together), the palette and accessible mode - larger click targets, strong focus rings, the high-contrast palette and reduced motion - from the next start. Knobs, faders, number fields, lists and buttons are reached with Tab and worked with the arrows, Page Up/Down and Home/End, and tell a screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver, Orca) what they are and their value - Help > Accessibility Check lists any control with no name, role or value, and the interface names them from their parameter or tooltip at start; the keyboard reaches every menu and most lists, and F6 moves between the panels.

Help > Ask answers a question from this manual and the command reference, offline: nothing is sent anywhere. Help > What's New shows the release notes, once per version at start; Help > Tours walks through the window part by part.

F1 (Help > Help for This) shows what the focused control is, the command it runs and its shortcut, above the manual opened at its section - no hovering needed. The manual has a find field (Enter: next match, Shift+Enter: previous); the Command Reference groups every command under these sections, names it as the menus do and finds it by a task ("mixing"), a shortcut or a word of its description. Help > Keyboard Shortcuts lists every shortcut by task; Enter runs the highlighted one. Lists - revisions, missing files, plug-ins in the project and the rest - have a find field, sort by a column title (numbers by value; a third click restores the list's own order), run their main action on Enter or a double-click and offer every action on the row from the Menu key, Shift+F10 or a right-click; each action's outcome stays in the Results log under the list.

Press Shift+F1 on any knob, fader, number or menu to learn its drag, wheel, keys and right-click options. Theme, zoom, palette and accessible-mode changes apply at the next start, and each change says so; a colour that makes text or a control fall below WCAG AA contrast is refused unless you allow low contrast.

Editing without a mouse. Every editing canvas has keys and an editable list. Note editor: Alt+Left/Right previous/next note (Shift adds), Alt+Up/Down the chord's note above/below, Home/End first/last; Ctrl+Left/Right shorten/lengthen, Ctrl+Up/Down velocity (Ctrl+Shift finely); Insert adds a note after the cursor; Enter opens its properties. Expression lane: Left/Right note to note, Up/Down value (Page Up/Down by 10). Controller lane: Left/Right a grid step, Ctrl+Left/Right point to point, Up/Down value at the cursor, Insert adds a point, Delete removes it. XY pad: arrows 1 % (Shift 10 %). Comp lanes: Up/Down lane, Left/Right beat (Ctrl bar), Shift marks a range, Enter comps it, C cycles the take. Arranger: Shift+Alt+Up/Down clip on the track above/below, Ctrl+Alt+Left/Right resize. Ctrl+Shift+L on any of these opens its list: a table you can read cell by cell and edit by typing (positions as 5.2.1, pitches as C5); Insert, Delete, + and - work there, and every edit is one undo step.

Announcements. Zene tells your screen reader about errors, refused edits, recording starting and stopping, and saving or opening a project, never meter movement; the announcements level (view.announcements) sets this to all, errors only, or off. With All it also says what you select (a clip with its track and bar/beat span, or a track or mixer channel and the edit cursor), the cursor's new position when you move it with the arranger's Left/Right/Home keys or Home in the note editor, the transport position when you start, stop, locate or scrub, and the name of a named marker you jump to or play across. Accessibility Check can run its checks separately: names, roles, values, keyboard reach and duplicate names.

Background tasks - renders from the render queue, stem splits, mastering, library indexing and background video-audio extraction - appear under TASKS in the footer. Click it (or press Space on it) to see each job's progress; Cancel stops the jobs that support it. The list never blocks the window.
