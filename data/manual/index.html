Zene Studio

Zene Studio is a music workstation in one window: the arrangement in the middle, the browser on the left, the Inspector on the right and the editors and the mixer below. Everything a click does is also a named command, so a script, an agent or a controller can do it too, and every edit can be undone.

Hover over a control to see what it does; press Shift+F1 (Help > What's This?) and click a control for its full description. Help > Command Reference lists every command with its arguments.

The window

  • Header - transport (play, stop, record), tempo, time signature, position and the loudness readout. Right-click it to choose which buttons it shows.
  • Browser - devices, presets, samples and projects. The Search source finds files across the library: words are combined, kick OR snare, -loop excludes, "bass drum" is a phrase, and tag: kind: name: path: ext: narrow a word. Save a search as a collection to keep it; Project Media lists every file the project uses.
  • Inspector - the selected track, clip or mixer channel: its settings, its sends and inserts, its take lanes.
  • Device panel - the devices of the selected track or channel as cards, with the rack's macro knobs.

Arranging

Drag clips to move them; drag their edges to trim. Choose a range (drag in the ruler) to split, delete, crop, fade or bounce it. Navigate holds the zoom, markers, ripple and overlap modes; Alt+arrows nudge and select clips. Sample clips show fade handles at their top corners and, with Navigate > Show Transients, their transients.

Editing notes

Double-click a MIDI clip to open it in the note editor. The tools draw, paint, select, cut and mute notes; the lanes below edit velocity, release velocity, chance and the clip's controller curves. The Notes menu quantises, transposes, scales, strums and transforms; Pitch Curve draws a bend on each note. Notes > Tools inverts, arpeggiates, flams, repeats, moves notes a degree up or down in the key, makes them follow the chord track, writes a Euclidean rhythm, moves notes to another track, splits a drum loop into a track per key and detects the key. Group keeps notes together when you select them; Lyrics lays syllables on the notes ("Hal-le-lu-jah"). Play On chooses the passes of the loop a note plays on; the Repeats lane plays a note several times inside its length; the Velocity Spread lane varies its velocity each time. Articulation picks one of the track's articulations (Track > Articulations... sets the keyswitch, program or controller each one sends). New Note Length fixes the length a click draws; Audio Behind the Notes shows an audio clip's waveform under them. The controller lane can also hold program changes.

Project > Global Swing delays every off-beat 16th (or 8th) of the song; Chase Notes sounds a note playback starts in the middle of; MIDI Playback Offset starts every note a little later, to line up a slow instrument.

Mixing

Each strip has its trim, polarity, mono and pan mode (balance, dual or width), sends with their own pan, inserts and a meter line with the clip light and the gain reduction. Right-click a strip for its Strip, Listen and Mixer View menus: show or hide sections, change the strip width, show only some channels, switch the meter between peak, RMS and pre-fader. VCA groups can be nested and choose which of level, mute and solo reach their members. Solo can be exclusive or additive (Mixer View > Solo Mode).

Devices

Add a device from the browser or the rack's menu; right-click a device to insert another before or after it, copy it or its whole chain to other channels, or compare two settings (A/B). Racks hold parallel chains, macros (each mapping with its own range and curve) and key or velocity zones; Wrap into Rack moves a chain into a rack chain. Plug-in windows have a header with bypass, A/B and presets. Favourite, hide or file plug-ins into folders from the effect picker or the plug-in manager (Project > Plug-in Manager).

Recording

Arm a track with the record button in its header (or Record > Arm All Audio Tracks), choose its input from its Record menu and press Record. Record > Count-In and Pre-Roll starts the take bars early; Record Mode chooses overdub or replace for MIDI. Every pass of a looped take becomes a take lane: drag along a lane in the take-lanes view to comp that range, double-click to cycle the take (or Record > Next Take Here). Retrospective capture keeps what you played without recording - save it as a clip or a take afterwards.

The launcher

Show the launcher from the header. Each cell plays a pattern or an audio file; drop a sample on a cell to fill it. Cells launch on the next bar (or the cell's own quantisation) in trigger, gate, toggle or repeat mode, and scenes launch a row. Launcher > Launch Scene 1-8 can be given keys (Settings > Shortcuts); Launcher > Controller binds the notes of a MIDI controller to cells and scenes. Launcher > Record What I Played Into a Cell puts what you just played (with Keep Listening on) into a cell as a new pattern.

Projects

Projects keep their history: Project > Revisions restores or compares an earlier state. Collect Media copies every file a project uses beside it; Project > Hand Off writes a folder someone else can open with a list of the devices to install first. Import Tracks copies tracks from another project; Save as Template keeps a project to start from.

Scripts, agents and controllers

Every command is reachable over the control socket (JSON-RPC), from Lua scripts (zene.command) and from the MCP bridge. A controller can drive the remote-control pages (eight knobs following the selected device), a Mackie Control surface, or any parameter through MIDI learn.

Access

Settings > Appearance sets the interface scale, the text size and a high-contrast palette (they take effect at the next start). Every control has a name for screen readers; the keyboard reaches every menu and list.
